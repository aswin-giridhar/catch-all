The problem

People lose money choosing a dropdown.

Sending crypto on the wrong network is one of the most common ways ordinary people permanently lose funds. The address looks equally valid on every chain, so the mistake is invisible until the money is gone.

Every existing fix is a warning label. A label puts the burden on the person least equipped to carry it.

The solution

Remove the dropdown.

There is no network to choose, so there is no network to get wrong. The recipient’s link accepts anything and always resolves to one predictable asset on one predictable chain.

Nothing is held

Money moves straight from payer to recipient in a single operation. No escrow contract, no relayer wallet, no custody.

No wrong network

Anything in, USDC on Arbitrum out. The chain is never a decision the user has to make.

No wallet needed

Whoever pays signs in with an email. Their account is created in the moment they need it.

How it works

Four moving parts, none of them visible.

MagicEmail login creates an embedded wallet. No install, no seed phrase.
EIP-7702The existing EOA is upgraded in place. Same address, no migration.
Universal AccountsOne balance across chains, with gas paid from any asset held.
ArbitrumEvery payment settles here, predictably and cheaply.

Two link types share one payment engine: an open receive link, and a paid link that releases something on payment.

Engineering

Measured, not assumed.

The payment path was audited before it ever touched mainnet, and every finding was checked against the SDK’s own type definitions rather than guessed at.

  • Delegation targets the chain funds come from, not the destination
  • chainId 0 is falsy but not nullish — ?? silently broke authorization signing
  • Authorizations deduped by (chain, nonce), because nonces are per-chain
  • Editing an amount invalidates the quote, so the screen can never show one figure and send another
  • Sends are ref-guarded, and a failed send is discarded rather than retried into a double payment
